# Rise to Ruin: vision for the later stages

Where the game is heading after Stage 1 (Stages 2 and 3, maybe 4). This file is the target that Stage 1 setup and every later proposal should aim at. It's a working draft, first written 2026-11-20 and fleshed out the same day. Nothing here is in the game yet.

**How to read this file:**
- **Yours** marks the author's own vision. Treat it as fixed until the author changes it.
- Everything else is a **suggestion** built on top of it. It sticks to what the vision and the Stage 1 plan ([STAGE1_PROPOSAL.md](STAGE1_PROPOSAL.md)) already set up, and every idea serves one of the venues below. Cut whatever doesn't fit.
- Scenes are described at plot level only. No scene is written here.

**Names:** design doc, so default names and role labels (Mom, Sister, Landlord, Boss, Manager, Prof). New side characters are archetypes without names. In the game every one of them gets a story variable.

**Ages:** every character is an adult. The college is a college. Its students are adult college students, never "pupils" in player-visible text and never school kids. The same goes for diner customers, shelter residents, the Landlord's friends and everyone else.

## 1. The big picture

**Yours:**
- The four side characters (Landlord, Manager, Boss, Prof) exist to give Mom and Sister **venues**, each with its own theme. More venues come later.
- **The player decides.** They end up at every venue because he sends them there.
- **The end state is the same everywhere.** Each venue escalates until Mom and Sister are sexually available to anyone there.
- **Every escalation has a cover story**: "to increase sales", "to help them focus", "it's your duty".

### 1.1 One sentence per stage

| Stage | Title (working) | What it's about |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | **A new Beginning** | The player builds his prototype and tries to survive moving back to his family. Over the stage, he manipulates everything to follow his vision. |
| 1 | **Rise** | He builds the company and corrupts them in private. The side lines are set up. He *offers* them, and only at the very end does he hand them over. |
| 2 | **The Rebuild** | Ethan took everything. He rebuilds, and each venue pays in something he needs. The others start *asking*. |
| 3 | **The Town** | He's back on top. The Prof becomes dean, the college opens up, new venues appear (the shelter, his own office). The venues start running on their own. |
| 4 | (optional) **Ruin** | Every venue at its end state. The investigation is over and the player is in control of everything. Ethan and the town are completely his and he can ruin whatever and whoever he wants. The title earns itself. |

### 1.2 Why the venues matter to the story: each one pays in a currency

Stage 1 ends with the company gutted: one device, a lawyer, an investigation, a rival in the press. In Stage 2 the player can't buy his way back, but he can trade. Each venue gives him one thing the rebuild needs, so the plot pushes the family deeper and the player always has a business reason he can tell himself.

| Venue | Currency | What it does for the rebuild |
|---|---|---|
| Landlord | **Property** | The building. A rent-free headquarters, and later the whole building with its tenants. |
| Manager | **Cash and a field lab** | A cut of the diner, and a public place to test the new device on strangers. |
| Boss | **Connections** | Contracts, introductions, his network. Eventually his company. |
| Prof / college | **Legitimacy** | The research cover that keeps the investigation off his back, and a steady supply of test subjects. |
| Shelter | **Reputation** | Good press while the investigation runs. A charity photo is worth a lot of Suspicion. |
| His own office | **Morale and efficiency** | Staff who stay, clients who sign. |

### 1.3 The ladder

Stage 1 defines levels 0 to 3 for the family option (3.1 of the proposal). The later stages need two more. Every venue climbs the same ladder in its own words.

| Level | Name | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Charm** | Dress code, coffee, small talk. |
| 1 | **Warm-up** | Flirting, touching, outfits chosen for someone. |
| 2 | **Entertain** | Being watched, being handled, stopping short of sex. |
| 3 | **Handed over** | Sex with someone the player picked, once. (End of Stage 1.) |
| 4 | **Kept** | A standing arrangement with the venue's owner (the Landlord's Saturdays, the Boss's Thursdays). Regular, expected, on the calendar. |
| 5 | **Open** | Anyone at the venue. The end state. |

- A venue's level is gated by her corruption stage, her exhibition and the venue's own quests. Mom and Sister climb each venue separately, so Sister can be at 4 at the diner while Mom is at 1 there.
- **Who asks** moves with the stages: in Stage 1 he offers, in Stage 2 the owner asks, in Stage 3 the venue *expects* and the player only says no (or doesn't).

### 1.4 The cover story ladder

The excuse matters as much as the act. It escalates too:
1. **A favor**: "Just help him out, he's lonely."
2. **The job**: "It's part of the shift now." / "It's in your contract."
3. **A duty**: "You're doing this for the family." / "For the company."
4. **Nobody explains it anymore.** The first scene where nobody bothers with an excuse is a beat of its own at every venue.

### 1.5 Mom and Sister are humiliated differently

- **Mom loses respectability.** She was the adult in every room: the competent one, the mother, the woman with the good job. Her venues take *authority* from her: the Boss's office where she used to work, the classroom where she's the teacher. Her lever stays "a mother's duty".
- **Sister loses status.** She's the popular, funny, bright one. Her venues take *reputation*: the diner where everyone knows her, the college where she's the most popular girl. Her lever is devotion to him: she does it because he asked.
- **Feelings** (proposal 3.6) color each venue. Disgust that doesn't stop her (Landlord), hatred that turns into something she won't name (Boss), a working relationship gone wrong (Manager), and with some of the students, something she enjoys and won't admit.

### 1.6 What the player sees

The formats from the proposal carry over: POV swaps behind the CCTV arrow, the end-of-day report, public-risk scenes. Later stages lean more on reports and POV, because he's sending them to more places than he can attend. The scenes he *does* attend are the milestones at each venue.

## 2. Landlord: old men and degradation

**Yours:** an old, ugly, disgusting man. Mom and Sister eventually exist for him to use and abuse, because the player decides they should. Old men, degrading scenes. Mostly in his home, also when his card buddies come over, and on outings like a walk in the park.

### The character later on
He's 76, lonely and petty, and owns the building. In Stage 1 he'd rather watch than ask. He turns from watching to asking once he realizes the player will say yes. His weakness is vanity: he wants his friends to *see* what he has. That's what pushes his venue from private to shared.

### Venues
- **His apartment.** Sticky, cluttered, the TV always on. The main stage. Domestic jobs (cleaning, cooking, laundry) turn into the frame for everything else.
- **Card night.** His buddies come over once a week. Background people first, then the stakes of the game.
- **Outings.** A walk in the park, the bench by the pond, the bus. Public scenes on his arm. Public risk raises Suspicion (section 8).

### Supporting cast (archetypes)
- **The loudmouth**: tells the same stories, bets big, gets the first turn.
- **The quiet widower**: says nothing, watches everything. The one Sister finds hardest to read.
- **The ex-military one**: barks orders, treats them like recruits.
Three is enough. Each one gives the card nights a different flavor without new venues.

### The ladder here

| Level | At the Landlord's | Cover story |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | The weekly "help" (Stage 1): shelves, tea, sitting with him while the show's on. | The rent deal. |
| 3 | The Saturday the player offers when the building goes up for sale. | "It's the building." |
| 4 | Saturdays are his. Housework in what he picks, the domestic routine as degradation. | "You're keeping house for him." |
| 4+ | Card night: first serving drinks, then the stakes. | "His friends are lonely too." |
| 5 | Outings, and anyone he brings. | None. |

### Mom and Sister here
- **Mom** is the "wife" he never had. The scenes are domestic: aprons, dinner, ironing, played as degradation.
- **Sister** is the trophy he shows his friends. Her scenes are about being passed around and talked about.

### Key beats (suggestions)
1. **The Offer** (Stage 2 opener): the player's answer to The Sale. He doesn't buy the building with money.
2. **The Deed**: the building is signed over, piece by piece, one milestone per floor.
3. **The First Card Night**: he invites the player too. The player declines and watches the CCTV instead. (The Landlord has his own cameras, from What He Saw. The footage is a currency of its own.)
4. **The Walk**: the first outing. A public-risk scene.
5. **The Rival Tenant returns** (from 7.1): she's still in the building and sees what's going on. Witness, rival, or the next resident of the venue. Author's call.
6. **The landlord's last day**: once the building is the player's, what happens to the old man? He stays as a tenant who now pays in a different way, or he's simply gone.

## 3. Manager: serving and exhibition

**Yours:** diner scenes. Sister (maybe later Mom) works in progressively lewder outfits and sexually serves any customer, because it's her duty, "to increase sales". The focus is serving and exhibition.

### The character later on
In Stage 1 he's not leering, he's counting. Later he does both. His arc is losing his own diner: the player takes a cut, then a bigger one, and the Manager ends up running the floor for the player. The small revenge from Stage 1 continues: he mops, he takes orders, he calls the player "Mr. Consultant", and then "sir".

### The venue
The diner, as it stops being a diner. The device makes it the field lab: the new Veil in the ceiling makes customers bolder and the staff more willing. It's the first public place where the player sees the new device work on strangers.

### Supporting cast (archetypes)
- **The truckers**: passing through, never the same twice. Good for anonymous service.
- **The construction crew**: the breakfast regulars, loud, in groups.
- **The old regular**: has had the same booth for twenty years, tips in coins, knew Sister when she started.
- **The detective** (optional, ties into Suspicion): the officer working the investigation becomes a regular. He gets compromised there. A very useful character for Stage 2.

### The ladder here

| Level | At the diner | Cover story |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | The new uniform (Stage 1): shorter, tighter, "the brand". | Sales. |
| 2 | Uniforms get lewder week by week. Table service gets friendlier. | "Tips are up." |
| 3 | Closing shifts on weekends (Stage 1's Closing Keys): private service after hours. | "Closing is a different job." |
| 4 | The back booth during opening hours, for regulars. A "special" on the menu that isn't food. | "The specials sell." |
| 5 | Serving anyone, out front. The diner is a destination. | None. |

### Mom and Sister here
- **Sister** first. Her bubbly service persona stays, and her jokes get thinner. The old regular who knew her from her first shifts is the one she can't look at.
- **Mom joins later**, as a hostess or "the new shift lead". The draw is mother and daughter on the same shift. The moment the regulars find out they're related is its own beat.

### Key beats (suggestions)
1. **Sales Report**: the Manager brings the numbers. Revenue tracks the uniform length. He asks for more without being asked.
2. **The Menu**: the specials card. Played as comedy at first.
3. **The Window Seat**: exhibition, seen from the street. Public risk.
4. **The Cut**: the player takes a bigger share of the diner, then the deed. The Manager signs.
5. **Family Shift**: Mom's first day.
6. **The Detective's Booth**: the investigation gets a blind spot.

## 4. Boss: office humiliation

**Yours:** humiliation in an office setting. "Bully boss" content.

### The character later on
In Stage 1 he's the one person the device doesn't work on (7.2). That's the point of him: everything he does, he does on his own. He's the only villain in the game who isn't steered, which makes him the cruelest one. He holds the clip, and later the player holds something on him.

### The venue
His office tower: the floor where Mom used to work (floor 6), and **"upstairs"**, his executive floor. Both matter. Upstairs is private, floor 6 is where her old colleagues sit.

### Supporting cast (archetypes)
- **His executives**: the yes-men he wants to impress. They're how the humiliation goes public.
- **Mom's old colleagues on floor 6**: the people who knew her as a competent manager. The most painful audience she has.
- **The woman who replaced her**: has Mom's old desk. Rival, witness, and possibly the next one he goes after.

### The ladder here

| Level | At the Boss's office | Cover story |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | On-site consultant (Stage 1): the dress code, his hands, the meetings. | The support contract. |
| 3 | After Hours, upstairs (end of Stage 1). | The Arrangement. |
| 4 | Thursdays are his. Assistant duties at her old workplace: coffee, filing, under the desk. | "Consulting hours." |
| 4+ | Performance reviews, run by him, about her, in front of his executives. | "Team building." |
| 5 | A reward for his best performers. The whole floor knows. | None. |

### Mom and Sister here
- **Mom** is the center. Her humiliation is losing authority in the one place that knew her as somebody.
- **Sister**: open question (section 9). One natural way in is an "internship" at a real company, arranged by the Boss as a favor.

### What the player gets
Connections at first, then contracts, then leverage, and in the end the Boss's company itself. **The reversal** (suggestion): the last beat of this line is the Boss working for the player. It's the player's revenge for every "kid".

### Key beats (suggestions)
1. **Upstairs**: the Stage 2 opener from 7.2.
2. **Floor 6**: the first time her old colleagues see her in her new role.
3. **The Review**: a performance review as a humiliation scene.
4. **The Clip, Again**: the player finally gets the clip back, or finds out he can't, and changes the terms another way.
5. **The Takeover**: the player buys, steers or blackmails his way into the company. The Boss becomes an employee.

## 5. Prof: the college

**Yours:** the Prof eventually becomes dean (maybe through murder) and reworks the college harshly. That gives the player more chances to send Mom and Sister there.
- **Sister** "tutors" students in increasingly lewd ways to "help them focus". She's the most popular girl at the college, so her reputation carries a lot at first, and she slowly falls entirely for the player. There are student groups (bullies, nerds, outsiders, ...) she has to help, to push the college's reputation. In the end she's used by anyone, like at every other venue.
- **Mom** starts working as a teacher. The focus is "rewarding" the students, and it turns into the same as Sister's, but with more humiliation, because she was the one with power as a teacher.

### 5.1 How the Prof becomes dean

The dean is set up in Stage 1 as a formidable minor character (7.4, the Dean's Visit). She wants a paper, she reads data carefully, and in Stage 2 she's the one person at the college who might ask what the Veil really does. She's in the way.

Ways to remove her (suggestions, in order of weight):
- **The Prof does it, the player covers it.** A late-night argument, a fall, a car. The player has the CCTV or the device logs and cleans it up. Now he owns the new dean completely. This is the strongest option: the college is the player's from day one.
- **The player does it with the device.** The device can't make anyone kill, but it can make a tired woman drive home at 2 a.m. without sleeping. Colder, and the player carries it alone.
- **A scandal instead of a death**, if murder turns out too much: the player feeds her something (the device, or a framed affair) and she resigns.
- Whatever the method, it's the darkest thing the player has done that isn't about the family. The investigation (Suspicion) should feel it.

### 5.2 The reform
The new dean's program, with names that sound good on paper:
- **The Mentorship Program**: mandatory tutoring for students below a grade line. Sister is its face.
- **Focus rooms**: study rooms with the Veil in the ceiling. The player's field lab with a waiting list.
- **The incentive system**: rewards for grades and attendance. The rewards become Mom's job.
- **A dress code for staff and tutors**, "for professionalism".
The college's reputation (graduation rates, rankings) is the number the dean shows the board. It goes up, and that's the cover.

### 5.3 Sister: the tutor

**The arc:** her popularity is a resource that she spends. She starts as the girl everyone wants to be tutored by. By the end her name means something different on campus, and she's fine with it because he's fine with it. "She falls entirely for the player": everything she gives up there, she gives up for him.

**Student groups** (each one is a short arc with its own flavor):

| Group | Who | Their flavor |
|---|---|---|
| **The athletes** (the bullies) | Loud, entitled, used to getting their way. | Aggression and being taken. They treat her like a prize. |
| **The nerds** | Awkward, polite, the STEM club. | Clumsy, eager, almost sweet. They worship her. (Mirrors her Stage 1 scene with Brian.) |
| **The outsiders** | The loners, the commuters, the students nobody notices. | Being *seen* for once. The group she might actually like. |
| **The student council / old money** (optional) | Polished, connected, careful. | Discretion, deals, favors for favors. The group with power over her reputation. |

Each group gets its own ladder: a first tutoring session, a "focus technique", the group session, the group itself. Each group she wins pushes the college's reputation up.

**Her friends** (the Friend, 6.6c in the proposal) are the audience that hurts. The friend who notices what the program really is gets a beat.

### 5.4 Mom: the teacher

**Her job:** the new dean hires her as an adjunct for a practical course she's actually qualified for: office management, project management, professional practice. Her real background makes the cover believable. For the first weeks she's genuinely good at it, and the students respect her.

**The reversal:** she grades, she sets deadlines, she sends people out of the room. The incentive system turns that around: the reward for good grades is her. The students she failed or scolded are the ones who collect first. Every scene is about the authority she had in that room and lost.

**The ladder:** strict teacher → the dress code → "office hours" → rewards for the top of the class → rewards for anyone who shows up → the end state.

### 5.5 Both on campus
Mother and daughter at the same college. The day the students find out they're related is a beat of its own, like at the diner.

## 6. More venues

### 6.1 The homeless shelter

**Yours:** Mom and Sister help out at a homeless shelter. It turns heavily sexual.

**How it comes in (suggestion):** as **PR during the investigation**. The company donates a "calm" unit to a shelter (the official product does exactly what it says: it calms people), and Mom and Sister volunteer there for the photos. The press loves it, Suspicion drops. It's the cheapest good headline the player can buy.

**Why it's its own flavor:** the lowest-status men in town and the family from the expensive house. Mom's warmth fits volunteering perfectly, which is what makes it cruel.

**Supporting cast:** the shelter director (earnest, overworked, grateful, never sees it), and residents as a group rather than individuals, with one or two regulars who stand out.

**The ladder:** serving meals → helping with hygiene and clothes → "giving comfort" → the night shift → the end state. The cover story is charity the whole way.

### 6.2 The player's own office

**Yours:** at his own office, they serve the staff and clients.

**How it fits (suggestion):** the Stage 2 or 3 headquarters (location 16 is the placeholder) is in the Landlord's building once the player owns it. Mom and Sister already work there.
- **Staff:** an "employee benefit". It plugs straight into **Morale** and **Efficiency** (the existing company stats): a good week is rewarded, and the staff who stay are the ones who've been rewarded.
- **Clients:** entertainment in the meeting room, closing deals. This is the "clients start asking" turn from the proposal (3.1), now in his own house.
- **Brian and Darius:** Brian's awkwardness and Darius's sharpness make them very different staff scenes. Whether either of them takes part is the author's call.

### 6.3 More to come
Further venues should follow the same pattern: a place, a cover story, a ladder, an end state, and a currency for the player. Places that are already in the game or set up are the natural candidates: the shop, the gym (the gym dinner in Stage 1), the supplier's club (the last night of Stage 1).

## 7. How the venues meet

The venues shouldn't stay separate worlds. Crossovers keep the town feeling like one place:
- The Landlord's card buddies start eating at the diner.
- The Boss sends his executives to the diner, or to the college as guest speakers.
- The athletes from the college come to the diner after games.
- The dean (the Prof) and the Boss sit on a committee together and compare notes.
- The shelter's residents get a meal at the diner once a week, a charity program run by the company.
- Mom and Sister meet at a venue where one of them didn't know the other was working.

## 8. Systems (suggestions)

What the existing code already covers, and the little that would be new:
- **Venue tasks.** Sending her to a venue is a task, the same way the Landlord's weekly visit and the diner shifts already are. Task `stages` already swap the activity and CCTV clip as a task repeats, which is exactly a venue's ladder.
- **Venue level.** One small state object per venue (for example `$venues.diner = { mom: 0, sister: 0 }`), raised by the venue's quests, read by its tasks and events. Gated by `setup.corruptionTotal`, exhibition and the corruption stage.
- **Wardrobe orders** (Stage 2 in the proposal) drive the uniforms and outfits at every venue.
- **Feelings** color each venue, per person (section 1.5).
- **POV and reports** show what he wasn't there for. Venues without cameras (the park, the shelter at night) only have the report.
- **Suspicion:** public venues (the park, the diner window, the college) raise it, the college's legitimacy and the shelter's PR lower it. The player has to balance the two.
- **Corruption stages:** venue levels are a good use for later stages (stage 2+ for Mom and Sister). The proposal's milestones (Willing, Complicit for Mom; Devoted, Mine for Sister) unlock levels 4 and 5.
- **Quest lines:** each venue owner already has (or gets) a line: `landlord_`, `manager_`, `boss_`, `prof_`. The shelter and the office can be quest lines on group objects, the way CLAUDE.md describes lines without a character.
